Heat Pump Rebates in 2026: What’s Left After the Federal Credit Ended

The federal 25C heat pump credit ended December 31, 2025. State HEAR/HOMES programs, utility rebates and local incentives remain — here’s how to find what you can actually claim in 2026.

7 min read Updated November 2026 Sources linked at the bottom

If a 2026 heat pump quote still subtracts a $2,000 federal tax credit, it is using a program that no longer exists. The 25C credit — up to 30% of project cost, capped at $2,000 for heat pumps — ended for systems placed in service after December 31, 2025. What remains is a patchwork of state and utility programs.

Verify before you sign State rebate programs launch, pause and run out of funds. Confirm the program is open, your address qualifies, and the equipment is on the approved list — before the installer orders equipment.

Example: New Jersey’s Whole Home rebates reach up to $7,500, with utility rebates of roughly $500–$1,400. Texas, by contrast, has not yet launched its HEAR rebates as of 2026. The difference between states is larger than the difference between heat pump brands.

How to check what you can claim

  1. State energy office — confirm whether HEAR/HOMES is open and whether your address or income qualifies.
  2. DSIRE — search federal, state and utility programs for your ZIP and technology.
  3. Your utility — ask about heat pump rebates, load-management programs and income tiers.
  4. Contractor quote — demand a line-item that does not assume the expired federal credit, and ask which programs they will file for.

Quote red flags

  • “$2,000 federal credit” still subtracted in 2026.
  • Rebate amounts promised before eligibility or funding is confirmed.
  • No mention of equipment eligibility lists (efficiency thresholds, HSPF2/SEER2 minimums).
  • Contract requires signing before rebate reservation — sequencing can forfeit eligibility.

Quick answers

Can I still get a federal heat pump tax credit for a 2025 install?

If the system was placed in service in 2025, you may still claim 25C on your 2025 return, subject to the rules in effect. Talk to a tax professional about your specific documentation.

Do heat pump rebates count as taxable income?

Utility rebates that reduce the purchase price generally are not taxable income for the homeowner. Tax treatment can vary by program type — confirm with a tax professional for large incentives.
