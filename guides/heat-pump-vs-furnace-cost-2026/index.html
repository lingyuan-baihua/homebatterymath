Heat Pump vs Furnace Cost in 2026: What You’ll Actually Pay

Heat pump vs gas furnace in 2026: annual heating costs depend on your fuel and electricity prices. See the math, equipment costs, and when a heat pump beats gas — plus what changed with incentives.

8 min read Updated November 2026 Sources linked at the bottom

The answer depends less on the equipment than on what you pay for gas and electricity. In a cheap-gas state, a heat pump may save only a couple hundred dollars a year on heating. In a high-priced-fuel or fuel-oil home, the same heat pump can cut heating bills by thousands — and it replaces the air conditioner too.

Worked example (New Jersey, 2026) Gas at $1.35/therm and a 95% AFUE furnace: about $1,100/year for heating. A heat pump at $0.26/kWh with COP 3.0: about $850/year — roughly $250 in heating savings. Add $300–$500/year from replacing the A/C, and the case improves; local rebates can push it further.

Climate and insulation shift total demand, but not the direction of the comparison. The two variables that flip the answer are the electricity-to-gas price ratio and how much auxiliary resistance heat the heat pump needs in cold weather.

The federal 25C credit ended for systems placed in service after December 31, 2025 — including the up-to-$2,000 heat pump credit. State and utility rebates remain, but availability varies widely by state and can change quickly. What still qualifies in 2026 →

When a heat pump is the better buy

  • You heat with propane, oil or electric resistance — fuel costs are high.
  • Your A/C is due for replacement — the heat pump does both jobs.
  • Your state or utility has a meaningful rebate, or you qualify for an income-based program.
  • Your climate is mild-to-mixed; cold-climate models can still work but need careful sizing.

When a furnace may still win: cheap natural gas, a mild climate with low cooling demand, or a constrained budget where the equipment premium can’t be recovered.

Quick answers

Do heat pumps work in cold climates?

Modern cold-climate heat pumps do, but efficiency falls as temperatures drop and auxiliary heat may kick in. Ductless cold-climate models are often specified with HSPF2 of at least 8.5 and SEER2 of at least 15.2. Sizing and insulation matter as much as the rating.

Is a heat pump cheaper than a gas furnace overall?

Not automatically. With cheap gas, heating-only savings can be modest — the stronger case is when the heat pump also replaces an aging A/C. A 15-year view usually favors the heat pump when fuel prices are high or rebates are meaningful.
