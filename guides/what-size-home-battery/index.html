What Size Home Battery Do You Need in 2026?

Most homes need 5–15 kWh of battery. Essentials backup runs 10–20 kWh; whole-home backup 20–40 kWh+. Here’s how to size from usage, critical load and your actual goal — not a sales quote.

7 min read Updated November 2026 Sources linked at the bottom

Battery size is a load question, not a house-size question. The median U.S. home uses about 29 kWh per day (EIA), but most homes only need 5–15 kWh of storage for bill shifting or essentials backup. Whole-home backup is where the numbers jump.

Quick ranges Bill shifting or evening solar: 5–15 kWh. Essentials backup: 10–20 kWh. Whole-home backup for 24 hours: roughly 20–40 kWh+, depending on A/C, heat and EV charging.

Size by the decision you’re making

GoalTypical battery sizeWhat it covers
Time-of-use bill shifting5–13.5 kWhEvening loads; 30% of daily use is a common planning share
Solar self-consumption10–15 kWhEvening use of daytime surplus; one 13.5 kWh unit fits many homes
Essentials backup10–20 kWhFridge, lights, router, medical equipment; hours to a day
Whole-home, 24 hours20–40 kWh+A/C, heat pump, well pump, EV charging drive the top end

The sizing formula

Start with the energy you need to carry through the period that matters: usable kWh = average load (kW) × hours you need to cover. Then divide by usable capacity and round up to a standard size:

Your loadHoursRaw needStandard size to plan for
1.2 kW essentials8 hours9.6 kWh10 kWh
3 kW half-house8 hours24 kWh27 kWh (or two units)
7.5 kW whole-home24 hours180 kWhNot practical — use a generator

That last row is the honest one: batteries are poor at multi-day whole-home coverage. If you need that, a generator or solar-plus-storage with recharging is the right tool. Compare backup options →

Four inputs that change the answer

  • Evening consumption. The battery only helps if the energy is used after the sun goes down.
  • Critical load. Measure what actually must run in an outage — not the whole panel.
  • Solar surplus. Storage can’t soak up more than your evening load can absorb; 30–50% of daily use is a practical planning range.
  • Usable capacity. Nameplate kWh is not delivered kWh; reserve capacity, efficiency and degradation shave the number down. Why usable kWh matters →
