NEM 3.0 Battery Savings in 2026: What Solar Owners Actually Keep

Under NEM 3.0, exported solar earns about $0.05–$0.08/kWh while on-site use offsets $0.30–$0.55/kWh. Here’s how much a battery adds, with the 2026 math and caveats.

7 min read Updated November 2026 Sources linked at the bottom

California’s net billing tariff — NEM 3.0 — changed what solar exports are worth. The short version: exported energy earns about $0.05–$0.08/kWh, while energy you use on-site offsets retail rates of roughly $0.30–$0.55/kWh. A battery is the machine that moves one to the other.

The one-line case A battery turns a ~$0.05 export into a ~$0.35–$0.39 bill saving. That gap is why California’s battery attachment rate reached roughly 94% in 2026.

How NEM 3.0 changes the math

  • Exports: credited at avoided-cost rates that vary by hour and season — roughly 5–8¢/kWh on average.
  • On-site use: avoids the full retail rate — roughly 30–55¢/kWh, highest in the evening.
  • Battery: stores midday solar for the evening, capturing the retail rate instead of the export credit.
  • Legal status: California courts upheld NEM 3.0 in March 2026; the policy is settled.

Add a local rebate, wider evening rates, or backup value and the case improves quickly.

How to size for NEM 3.0

  • Start with evening usage, not array size. The battery’s job is covering the expensive hours.
  • One 13.5 kWh battery covers a typical California home’s 7–12 kWh evening/critical-load profile.
  • If you have an EV or electric heat, plan for more capacity or expect to import during long evenings.
  • Check your rate plan: the difference between peak and off-peak prices sets the ceiling on savings.

Quick answers

Does NEM 3.0 apply to existing solar owners?

Existing NEM 2.0 customers generally keep their original terms for a set period. New systems and new applications are placed on the net billing tariff. Check your utility’s interconnection agreement before assuming which regime you are on.

Is a battery required under NEM 3.0?

No — solar works without one. But without a battery, most of your generated value is exported at low avoided-cost rates. The battery is optional hardware with an increasingly central economics role.
