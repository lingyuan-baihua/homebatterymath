How many kWh do you actually need?

sizing by the decision you’re making — not a sales quote

Battery size is a load question, not a house-size question. The median U.S. home uses about 29 kWh per day, but most homes only need 5–15 kWh of storage for bill shifting or essentials backup. Whole-home backup is where the numbers jump. Pick your goal below, then use the calculator above to confirm against your actual usage and critical load.

GoalTypical battery sizeWhat it covers
Time-of-use bill shifting5–13.5 kWhEvening loads; ~30% of daily use is a common planning share
Solar self-consumption10–15 kWhEvening use of daytime surplus; one 13.5 kWh unit fits many homes
Essentials backup10–20 kWhFridge, lights, router, medical gear — hours to a day
Whole-home backup, 24 hours20–40 kWh+A/C, heat pump, well pump, EV charging drive the top end

The sizing formula behind the calculator

Usable kWh = average load (kW) × hours you need to cover, then round up to a standard size (5 / 10 / 13.5 / 20 / 27 kWh). A 1.2 kW essentials load for 8 hours needs ~10 kWh; a 3 kW half-house for 8 hours needs ~27 kWh; a 7.5 kW whole-home load for 24 hours would need 180 kWh — at which point a generator is the honest answer, not a battery.

How long a 13.5 kWh battery lasts

Runtime = usable capacity ÷ what you keep running. Same 13.5 kWh battery that powers essentials for a day will run a whole home with A/C for under two hours — the load is the variable.

Critical loadWhat it coversRuntime on 13.5 kWh
0.5 kWFridge, LED lights, router, phones~27 hours
1 kWEssentials plus a TV or small appliance~13 hours
3 kWHalf the house~4.5 hours
7.5 kWWhole home without A/C~1.8 hours

Ideal-case math. Real-world runtime is 10–20% shorter due to inverter efficiency, surge loads (fridge and well-pump compressors draw several times their running power at startup), temperature and battery age. Round up if you need guaranteed runtime.

What changes your number

  • Evening consumption — the battery only helps if energy is used after sundown.
  • Critical load — measure what must run in an outage, not the whole panel.
  • Usable vs nameplate — a “13.5 kWh” battery may deliver less; reserve and efficiency shave the number. See why usable kWh matters →
  • Solar surplus — storage can’t soak up more than your evening load can absorb.
