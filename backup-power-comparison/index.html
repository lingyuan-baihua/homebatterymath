Buy a home battery if…

You want silence, instant switchover and zero running cost — and your outages are hours, not days. Also stores your solar power every day, so it earns its keep between storms.

Buy a standby generator if…

Outages in your area last days, or you need to run big loads (well pump, A/C, stove). Piped natural gas = unlimited runtime. Trade-offs: noise, fumes, weekly exercise and yearly service.

Buy a power station if…

Budget is tight, you rent, or you also camp/RV. Silent and indoor-safe, but small capacity — it covers essentials for a night, not a whole house for days.

How this comparison works (methodology & assumptions)
Runtime (battery/station) = capacity (kWh) ÷ critical load (kW)
Propane use ≈ 0.21 gal per kWh delivered · Natural gas ≈ 0.20 therm per kWh
Generator fuel cost/hr = load × rate × local fuel price
10-year cost = upfront + 10 × (fuel per outage × outages/yr + maintenance)
Cost per backed-up hour = 10-year cost ÷ (hours actually covered × 10)
  • Fuel rates are grounded in typical specs: a 7 kW unit uses ≈1.5 gal propane/h and ≈140 ft³ natural gas/h at full load; we scale with your actual critical load.
  • Generator covers the full outage on piped natural gas; propane is limited by your tank size. It must be rated ≥ your critical load.
  • Battery & station discharge until empty; recharging from the grid before the next outage is assumed.
  • Not modeled: solar recharging, battery depth-of-discharge limits, propane delivery during disasters, transfer-switch permitting costs, resale value.
  • Default installed costs are 2026 U.S. mid-market reference points — battery ≈ $14,500 (13.5 kWh installed), standby generator ≈ $9,000 (8 kW installed class). Replace them with your local quotes.
  • Prices are typical installed/market ranges (US reference — FX updated 2026-11-06); verify locally.
