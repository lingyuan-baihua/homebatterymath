Net metering & exports

nem3 NEM 3.0: exports paid at avoided cost, not retail

  • Export compensation: Avoided-cost export credits that vary by hour — lowest at midday, highest during the summer evening peak.
  • Export rate: Representative NEM 3.0 export values: midday ~$0.05/kWh (10 a.m.–3 p.m.), evening peak ~$0.15/kWh (4–9 p.m.), overnight ~$0.03/kWh — from the CPUC Avoided Cost Calculator. Exact hourly values change over time.
  • Grandfathering: NEM 2.0 customers keep their 20-year agreement terms; new systems enroll under NEM 3.0 (the Net Billing Tariff).
  • What's ahead: Export values follow the CPUC Avoided Cost Calculator and drift over time — do not assume today's evening peak value holds for 20 years.

Battery incentives in California

Federal 30% credit: ended The 30% Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) ended for systems placed in service after Dec 31, 2025 (OBBBA). A cash/loan battery installed in 2026 gets $0 federal credit. Leases/PPAs may still earn a 30% credit under Section 48E, but it goes to the system owner, not you. IRS — Residential Clean Energy Credit ↗
State rebate
SGIP closed to the general market; income-qualified RSSE remains

The general-market SGIP budgets (Small Residential Storage and Equity Resiliency) are closed to new applicants. The state-funded RSSE program (AB 209) is open to income-qualified households, with waitlists in most territories. Municipal utilities run their own rebates — see below.

What a battery is worth in California

Strategy: Self-consumption

Under NEM 3.0, daytime solar is worth little when exported but worth $0.30–0.55/kWh when used on-site. A battery that shifts solar into the 4–9 p.m. peak cuts the biggest part of the bill, and the same battery covers PSPS and wildfire outages.

Cost context A typical 13.5 kWh system runs $10,000–$16,500 installed before incentives (national range).

Reference range: solar-plus-battery payback in California runs roughly 6–9 years under NEM 3.0 — it depends on your rate plan, usage and system size. Run the calculator with your own numbers instead of assuming this range. Is a home battery worth it? ↗

Watch out General-market SGIP is closed and the federal credit is gone, so incentives are thinner than in past years. Export rates decline over time — model self-consumption, not export revenue.

What's changing in California

Policy, rates and utility news that affect home batteries. Updated 2026-11-20.

What changed

  • Aug 7, 2026
    Utility medium

    California battery storage surpasses 21,112 MW

    The California Energy Commission reported total battery storage capacity of 21,112 MW — growth of more than 2,500% in seven and a half years, the country's largest battery fleet.

    Why it matters: Scaled storage supports grid reliability and signals a mature storage market that residential batteries plug into over time.

    California Energy Commission — California surpasses 21,000 MW of battery resources ↗
  • Jul 15, 2026
    Event high

    PG&E prepares a PSPS for portions of 10 counties

    Monitoring a high-wind event, PG&E prepared a possible Public Safety Power Shutoff across portions of 10 counties, with around 7,800 customers potentially affected beginning July 15, 2026.

    Why it matters: Wildfire-season PSPS outages are the core backup motivation for a California battery — this event is a concrete example of the risk.

    PG&E — investor relations, PSPS for portions of 10 counties ↗
  • Jul 2, 2026
    Policy medium

    CPUC adopts energy storage foldback rules

    In D.26-07-008 (adopted July 2, 2026) the CPUC clarified and modified qualification rules for energy storage, addressing foldback — where a battery's max and min power decline near its state-of-charge limits.

    Why it matters: Batteries participating in CAISO market services face new discharge behavior rules — relevant if you rely on grid-service or VPP revenue.

    CPUC — fact sheet on the Track 1 final decision (D.26-07-008) ↗
  • Mar 9, 2026
    Policy medium

    California Court of Appeal upholds the CPUC Net Billing Tariff

    On March 9, 2026 the California Court of Appeal upheld the CPUC's Net Billing Tariff (the NEM 2.0 successor adopted in 2022), confirming its legal standing.

    Why it matters: The net-billing tariff's legal status is settled — NEM 3.0 export terms stand, reducing policy uncertainty for new battery installs.

    Stoel Rives — energy regulatory update (March 25, 2026) ↗
  • March 2026
    Rates medium

    PG&E restructures the electric bill with a Base Services Charge

    PG&E debuted a restructured electric bill in March 2026, introducing a Base Services Charge of about $24 per month for most residential customers.

    Why it matters: A new fixed monthly charge changes the bill structure — factor it into any battery payback model for PG&E territory.

    PG&E — Currents, restructured electric bill debuts in March 2026 ↗
  • Dec 31, 2025
    Policy high

    Federal 25D battery credit ends

    The 30% Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) ended for systems placed in service after Dec 31, 2025.

    Why it matters: California buyers lost the federal credit on top of the closed general-market SGIP — incentive stacking is now much thinner.

    IRS — Residential Clean Energy Credit ↗
  • Dec 31, 2025
    Incentives high

    SGIP general-market budgets close to new applicants

    The Small Residential Storage and Equity Resiliency SGIP budgets stopped accepting new applications. The income-qualified RSSE (AB 209) budget remains open with waitlists in most territories.

    Why it matters: The state's main residential storage rebate is no longer available to general-market customers — model payback without it unless you qualify for RSSE.

    SGIP — Program Metrics and Budget Tracker ↗
  • Dec 18, 2025
    Policy medium

    CPUC sets 2026–2028 cost-of-capital and authorized ROEs

    In D.25-12-043 the CPUC authorized PG&E, SDG&E and SoCalGas with ROEs just below 10 percent and SCE slightly above 10 percent for the 2026–2028 period.

    Why it matters: Cost-of-capital is a baseline input to future rate cases — it shapes the rate trajectory a battery ROI is modeled against.

    CPUC — Cost of Capital ↗
  • Sep 29, 2025
    Market medium

    PG&E unveils a $73B grid plan tied to data-center demand

    PG&E filed a $73 billion transmission and grid-modernization plan covering 2026–2030, with a data-center demand pipeline of about 12.7 GW — up from earlier estimates.

    Why it matters: Data-center load can push rates and grid investment higher over time, shaping the long-term rate structure a home battery saves against.

    Reuters — PG&E unveils $73B spending plan to meet surging data-center energy demand ↗
