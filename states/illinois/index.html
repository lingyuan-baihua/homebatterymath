Net metering & exports

supply-only New systems since 2025 earn supply-only credits; pre-2025 legacy keeps full retail

  • Export compensation: Excess generation is credited at the supply rate only — no delivery, taxes or fees — for interconnections approved after December 31, 2024.
  • Export rate: Ameren's supply credits do not expire and carry over until used; check your ComEd tariff for credit expiry rules.
  • Grandfathering: Legacy customers — systems that completed interconnection approval, installation and PTO by December 31, 2024, with all net-metering paperwork received by 5 p.m. CPT that day — keep full-retail credits (supply + delivery + taxes/fees). Accepting a new DG rebate or modifying the system can give up legacy status.
  • What's ahead: No change ahead: the supply-only framework took effect January 1, 2025 and is already the rule for new systems. If you are on legacy terms, confirm any rebate trade-off with your utility first.

Battery incentives in Illinois

Federal 30% credit: ended The 30% Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) ended for systems placed in service after Dec 31, 2025 (OBBBA). A cash/loan battery installed in 2026 gets $0 federal credit. Leases/PPAs may still earn a 30% credit under Section 48E, but it goes to the system owner, not you. State rebate
$300/kWh battery rebate through Illinois Shines

Illinois Shines pays $300/kWh for residential battery storage (about $4,050 on a 13.5 kWh system), plus $300/kW for a smart inverter, in ComEd and Ameren territory. Systems must stay interconnected and operating for 5 years. Pre-2025 legacy net-metering customers should ask ComEd/Ameren how taking the rebate affects their full-retail credits.

What a battery is worth in Illinois

Strategy: Self-consumption

The $300/kWh rebate cuts the installed cost by roughly a third, which is the main reason a battery pencils out here. With supply-only credits for new systems, the savings come from using your own solar (or cheap overnight power) instead of exporting it.

Cost context A typical 13.5 kWh system runs $10,000–$16,500 installed before incentives (national range).
Watch out Legacy full-retail customers can lose their status by accepting a DG rebate — model both paths before applying. There is no published residential TOU data in our set, so arbitrage math needs your actual tariff.

    Net metering flips to supply-only for new systems
