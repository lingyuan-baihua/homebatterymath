How this calculator works (methodology & assumptions)

Under NEM 3.0 (the Net Billing Tariff), exported solar is credited at the CPUC Avoided Cost Calculator value — very low midday (≈5¢/kWh) — instead of the near-retail credit of NEM 2.0. A battery captures that midday surplus and discharges it during the 4–9 pm peak, displacing grid import at the full retail on-peak rate:

Value per kWh stored = retail on-peak − midday avoided-cost export
Daily solar surplus = kWp × 4.5 peak-sun-hours × 55% export share
Solar arbitrage = min(surplus, capacity × cycles) × efficiency × (peak − avoided cost)
TOU arbitrage (no solar) = capacity × cycles × efficiency × (peak − off-peak)
Degradation: capacity(y) = capacity × [1 − (1 − EOL%) × y / lifespan]
Payback = first year where cumulative savings ≥ net cost (interpolated)
  • NEM 3.0 arbitrage (with solar): each stored kWh is worth the difference between the retail on-peak rate and the midday avoided-cost export credit — the core "6–10×" uplift that makes batteries valuable under NEM 3.0.
  • TOU arbitrage (no solar, or remaining battery capacity): charge off-peak, discharge at peak; value = peak − off-peak.
  • Round-trip efficiency (default 90%) accounts for charging/discharging losses.
  • Degradation declines linearly from 100% to your warranty floor (default 70%) over the lifespan; yearly savings scale with remaining capacity.
  • Export share of 55% assumes roughly half of midday solar is self-consumed during the day, the rest is surplus available to store or export.
  • Taxes, demand charges, ACC Plus adders and financing costs are excluded.
